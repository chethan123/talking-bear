Loopback click latency and echo tail

X-E5 (plan §5): Start plays 20 clicks, 1.5 s apart (about 30 s), through the app's master bus (gain 0.7) and finds each one in this phone's own mic, through the app's audio engine: the mic opens with the app's constraints (echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain all off) and each click is cut from the capture worklet's ring with a take. Put the phone on a table in an ordinary room, set the media volume the app will use, keep quiet, and press Start.

Per click: arrivalLagMs is the measured output plus input latency (the first sample after the click's scheduled time T above 10× the RMS of the take's first 50 ms); predictedOutMs is the output latency that getOutputTimestamp() predicts at schedule time; residualMs is their difference, the input latency plus the prediction error. A small, stable residual (p90 − p10 ≤ 5 ms) validates the audio side of clipTimeNow (plan §3.3); the verdict needs at least 10 detected clicks. The spread can't show a constant prediction error: that is inside the median residual, together with the input and acoustic latency. tailMs is the echo tail: the time from the arrival until the level stays within 3 dB of the pre-click noise floor for 50 ms. It sets the cooldown (plan §4.4 point 3): max(250, p90 of tailMs + 50) ms. A tail that hasn't settled by the end of its take counts at the largest tail that take could show, and the cooldown is then a lower bound. The log also gives each click's raw getOutputTimestamp() and marks the clicks whose prediction fell back to currentTime − outputLatency.

Privacy (plan Q2): each take is analysed as soon as it arrives, then zero-filled and dropped; only the numbers are kept. Nothing captured is stored or exported; Copy log copies text only.
